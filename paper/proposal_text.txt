3 Statement of the problem 
Indian banks do not adhere to a unified format for bank statements. They employ a variety 
of layouts, table structures, narration styles, and field conventions. This heterogeneity in the 
representation of transaction strings, combined with often noisy and unstructured 
transaction descriptions, makes automated credit assessment a significant challenge [1]. 
As a result, lenders often remain reliant on manual reviews, rule-based parsing techniques, 
and costly third-party orchestrators. This dependency not only delays the loan processing 
timeline but also hinders scalable underwriting operations. Furthermore, because banks rely 
on these third-party applications, there are inherent privacy risks and additional costs 
involved. Additionally, most credit-risk models remain static or are only periodically updated, 
which makes it difficult for them to adapt to the growing volume and complexity of business 
transactions. 
Understanding the payee information within transaction strings is crucial for credit-risk 
modeling. Although many existing credit models utilize this information, they often remain 
static or only update periodically [2]. This static nature limits their ability to keep pace with 
the increasing volume and complexity of business transactions. 
One key challenge is that non-merchant transactions typically offer little to no added value 
for risk assessment. Moreover, there can be multiple payees with the same name, which 
complicates the process of identifying merchant-related transactions.

In our simulation studies, we discovered that only about 10% of transactions are merchant 
transactions. This makes it essential to classify payees into merchant and non-merchant 
categories. To achieve this, we use a fine-tuned SLM or a trained classifier, with an 
emphasis on high recall to ensure that we accurately identify as many merchant payees as 
possible. 
Our model is designed to be lightweight so that it can be easily deployed in local computing 
environments. Instead of relying on costly and resource-intensive Google searches to find 
payee information, we leverage a Large Language Model’s knowledge base for known 
merchants. As a fallback, we use Langsearch [3], a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) 
[4] system based on internet data, which is both fast and cost-effective while providing 
accurate information. 
Additionally, most of the machine learning models have issues of data leakage on Inference, 
this problem also exists with credit risk models. To address this challenge, we introduce an 
on-device differential privacy–enhanced small language model (DP-SLM) [5], inspired by 
the differential privacy framework for LLMs (DPF-LLM) [6]. In addition, we propose a 
distributed LLM architecture in which lightweight models operate collaboratively across

4 Objectives / Solution 
1. Efficient extraction of Bank transaction information from heterogeneous bank 
statements. 
Given the heterogeneity of Indian bank statements, the system must reliably extract 
the maximum amount of relevant transaction information while effectively managing 
differences in formatting, structure, and presentation. 
 
2. Efficient extraction of payee name and merchant/non merchant classification. 
Payee names in transaction strings provide important insight into a borrower’s 
financial behavior. Since banks use different transaction-string formats, accurate 
extraction of payee names is essential. Because most transactions are non-merchant 
and add limited value, the system must also classify merchant vs. non-merchant 
transactions with very high recall for merchant detection. 
 
3. Making Credit risk detection models dynamic by adding real time merchant 
information. 
Most credit-risk models are static or updated only periodically, making them unable 
to capture newly emerging merchants. Therefore, incorporating real-time merchant 
information is essential to keep the models dynamic and responsive. 
 
4. Ensure user privacy throughout the app, and credit risk models. 
Financial data is highly confidential, and credit-risk models can leak training 
information during inference. Therefore, strong privacy protections must be 
integrated throughout the system’s design and development. 
 
5. Store resolved merchant information for faster processing in the future 
 After a merchant is identified once, their details are stored in a local merchant 
database. This avoids repeated LLM or Langsearch calls for the same merchant, 
improving response time and reducing computational costs. 
 
6. Introduce a distributed LLM architecture for security and low latency 
To ensure scalability, a distributed computing architecture must be implemented, and 
a distributed LLM setup built on this paradigm should be used.

5 Product / Solution Category of the proposed project 
Improvements to existing Products with significant value add:  
Qualifying Features: Most existing credit risk models are largely static and, even when 
dynamic, are updated only periodically, causing them to miss newly emerging businesses. 
With the rapid growth in transaction volume and business diversity, credit risk models must 
become more adaptive. We propose a dynamic pipeline that incorporates real-time 
merchant information extracted directly from transaction data. Merchant identification is 
performed using a Large Language Model (LLM) knowledge base with a LangSearch 
fallback for real-time retrieval. Transactions are categorized into 20 merchant categories, 
improving merchant classification accuracy from 49% to 91%. Additionally, privacy risks are 
mitigated by training models using Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (DP-
SGD), ensuring strong privacy guarantees during training and inference. We have already 
made initial prototype of CreditMitra as shown in Fig. 2, where users upload 
transaction data in an unstructured format, which is then automatically analyzed and 
categorized.
